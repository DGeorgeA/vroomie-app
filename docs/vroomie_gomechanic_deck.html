A bearing that fails the week after a service goes unnoticed until it is loud, expensive or roadside.

04Fuel-quality screening

Listening for the sounds bad fuel makes.

Adulteration and ethanol-blend mismatch are live consumer anxieties in India. Vroomie screens for the engine sounds associated with fuel-quality problems and routes the customer to inspection.

Indicator 01

Piston / engine knock

The best-established audible symptom — wrong octane or water-contaminated fuel disturbs combustion timing.

Indicator 02

Power steering pump whine

Distinct squeal-family acoustic signature.

Indicator 03

Rocker arm / valve noise

Valvetrain sounds accompanying poor combustion.

What it says to the customer

"Possible relevant indicators detected" — the actual sounds found, followed by a recommendation to have the fuel system inspected by a qualified workshop.

Every result carries a visible disclaimer that this is an AI audio screening, not a laboratory fuel test, and that similar sounds have causes unrelated to ethanol.

Why the restraint is the selling point

The feature never declares fuel contaminated. It reports what it heard and sends the car to a technician — which is exactly the behaviour that makes it safe for a service network to put its name on.

A screening tool that cried "contaminated fuel" would be indefensible the first time a lab test disagreed.

For GoMechanic: fuel worry is one of the few reasons a driver checks a car that is running fine — converting diffuse anxiety into a bookable inspection. Admin-controlled; switchable network-wide without a release.
Detection figures measured on held-out recordings the system never saw during development. Current limits stated plainly: nine fault families supported; roughly one in twenty-three healthy cars may see a low-confidence alert; large-scale validation across handset models is the next milestone.
